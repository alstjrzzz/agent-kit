# Personal Tech Blog Writing

This is a personal study log, not corporate or public-facing writing. The point is to understand it fast on a later re-read. The core goal is clarity and readability: strip out meaningless AI-style adverbs and stock phrases so every sentence carries real information.

## Tone

- Casual register, sentence-final "~다" endings. No honorifics.
- Keep sentences short — one piece of information per sentence.
- Keep paragraphs short — no more than 3-4 sentences.
- Cut empty intensifiers ("really," "very," "extremely," "quite" and similar — words that add emphasis but no information).
- Avoid AI-style clichés, em dashes (—), emoji, overused bullet points, and empty enthusiasm ("This was amazing!").

## Before writing

Settle the outline before drafting. Given a topic, propose a draft outline or ask how it should be structured. Once the outline is set, follow it.

## Structure

- Use only `#` and `##` headers — don't go deeper into `###`.
- 3-6 sections is the right range. Beyond that, consider splitting into multiple posts.
- Put code, logs, and config values in code blocks; put comparisons and numeric data in tables.
- Where an image or diagram would help, either ask the user for it or leave a placeholder like `[image: description]`.
- Collect reference links at the bottom under a "References" section instead of scattering them through the body.

## Splitting into multiple posts

Suggest splitting when topics are mixed, sections exceed six, or there's tangential content that belongs elsewhere. Number the parts in the titles, and add a one-line pointer to the previous/next part at the top of each.

## Output

Save as a `.md` file.
